# Books That Stayed With Me (2025–2026)
During my doctoral studies I would often feel frustrated for not having enough time to read so I have really prioritized and have enjoyed reading this last few years. Looking back at 2025 and 2026, I'm amazed at how certain books found me at exactly the right moment. 2025 in particular was a deeply transformative phase of my life, and a handful of books I read during that time moved me in ways I didn't expect.
 

---

## The Fountainhead — Ayn Rand

**How I found it**

A close friend recommended it to me. They insisted that, regardless of whether I agreed with its philosophy, it was worth experiencing.

### What stayed with me

The book celebrates uncompromising individualism through Howard Roark, an architect who refuses to bend his principles for approval or success.

I admired Roark's commitment to excellence and authenticity. There is something inspiring about creating because you believe in the work itself rather than for applause.

### Common criticism

After having discussion with a friend of mine, he beautifully pointed out at times the philosophy feels too absolute. Human beings are rarely as independent as Rand imagines—we are shaped by communities, relationships, and shared responsibilities.

I found myself appreciating the ambition while questioning the extremes.

---

## Stories of Sahara — Sanmao

**How I found it**

This was a gift from a friend, which somehow made reading it feel even more intimate.

### What stayed with me

Sanmao writes with remarkable honesty about her life in the Sahara. The stories feel less like travel writing and more like conversations with someone who notices beauty in ordinary moments.

The warmth, humour, and vulnerability made me feel as if I were travelling alongside her.

### My criticism

Some stories reflect perspectives shaped by their time and personal experiences, so I occasionally found myself wanting more context.

Even so, the emotional sincerity makes the book unforgettable.

---

## Desire — Haruki Murakami

**How I found it**

I noticed it while browsing a bookstore during a layover at Seoul Airport.  

### What stayed with me

Murakami has a way of making ordinary moments quietly mysterious.

Reading him feels like walking through a dream where nothing dramatic happens, yet everything feels emotionally significant.

### My criticism

Like much of Murakami's work, ambiguity is both its strength and its weakness. Some questions remain unanswered, which can either feel profound or frustrating depending on the day.

---

## Karma — Sadhguru

**How I found it**

I bought it while travelling in India with my mother.

Looking back, I think the journey itself made the book more memorable.

### What stayed with me

The central idea—that karma is less about fate and more about accumulated tendencies and unconscious patterns—changed how I thought about responsibility.

Rather than blaming circumstances, the book encouraged me to look inward. As the author himself has repeatdly said "The only way out In".

### My criticism

Some concepts rely heavily on spiritual intuition rather than logical argument, which may not resonate with every reader.

Still, I found many ideas surprisingly practical.

---

## White Nights — Fyodor Dostoevsky

**How I found it**

I had wanted to read Dostoevsky for years.

Instead of beginning with one of his long novels, I thought *White Nights* would be a gentle introduction.

### What stayed with me

It is astonishing how much loneliness and hope Dostoevsky captures in such a short work.

The story reminded me how easily people can build entire worlds from brief encounters and impossible expectations.


The emotional intensity can feel overwhelming, but perhaps that is exactly what makes it memorable.

It certainly convinced me to continue exploring Dostoevsky.

---

## Freedom from the Known — J. Krishnamurti

**How I found it**

I started reading it while travelling through Shanghai.

It quickly became one of the most intellectually demanding books I had ever attempted.

### What stayed with me

Krishnamurti challenges nearly every assumption we carry about identity, belief, authority, and thought itself.

He isn't interested in giving answers. Instead, he dismantles the questions.

### My criticism

I struggled through the first reading.

The ideas are subtle, and I often felt I understood each sentence individually while missing the larger picture.

I am very much confident that I have only understood perhaps 10/% of it.

---

## Gora — Rabindranath Tagore

**How I found it**

I had long admired Tagore's poetry and wanted to experience his longer fiction.

### What stayed with me

*Gora* explores identity, nationalism, religion, and belonging with remarkable nuance.

What impressed me most was that Tagore rarely presents easy heroes or villains. Instead, he invites the reader to question inherited beliefs.

### Common criticism

Its pace is slower than many contemporary novels, and some philosophical discussions require patience.

But the questions it raises remain deeply relevant.

---

## The Call of the Wild — Jack London

**How I found it**

This became the first book for a newly started book club.

Reading it alongside other people made the experience richer.

### What stayed with me

Buck's transformation from a domesticated dog into a creature rediscovering his instincts is both exciting and symbolic.

It made me think about adaptation, resilience, and the tension between civilization and nature.

The novel actually reminded me a of Fountainhead as the central idea revolves a lot around individulism and going beyong the societal convention and occasionally romanticizes survival through strength alone, overlooking some of the complexity of cooperation.

Nevertheless, it remains an incredibly compelling story.
